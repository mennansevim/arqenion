# Arqenion VR — Open Appendectomy Technician Simulation
## Revised Promotional Video Prompt Pack

## 1. Production Concept

- **Format:** One continuous promotional composition divided into 8 connected scenes
- **Total duration:** 64 seconds
- **Scene duration:** 8 seconds each
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 widescreen
- **Recommended output:** 1920 × 1080, 24 fps
- **Primary viewpoint:** First-person view of a 20–25-year-old scrub surgical technician
- **Procedure scope:** Open appendectomy preparation, sterile workflow, team awareness and one representative instrument-support moment only
- **Role boundary:** The surgeon performs the operation. The technician prepares, protects and organizes the sterile field, monitors the workflow, anticipates requests, selects instruments and supports safe transfer. The technician never incises, dissects, retracts tissue, handles exposed anatomy or performs the surgeon’s task.
- **Visual tone:** Photorealistic premium medical training platform; calm, credible and educational rather than game-like
- **Patient visibility:** Adult training patient fully covered by opaque sterile drapes; no incision, wound, blood or exposed anatomy
- **Text workflow:** Generate blank UI surfaces and icon-only indicators. Add all readable text in post-production.

## 2. Narrative Spine

This is not a collection of independent clips. It is one uninterrupted experience:

**physical training room → headset transition → scrubbed first-person orientation → vital-sign awareness → instrument-table preparation → sterile safety check → surgeon request and safe support → performance feedback and Arqenion VR brand reveal**

Every scene must inherit the exact spatial arrangement, lighting direction, hand position, sound bed and last camera direction of the previous scene. No reset, teleportation, unexplained location change or new instrument layout is allowed between scenes.

## 3. Global Continuity Prompt

Add this paragraph unchanged to every video-generation prompt:

> Same 22-year-old scrub surgical technician throughout, youthful but professional appearance visible only in the opening reflection, same teal surgical attire, same sterile blue surgical gown, same correctly fitted cap and mask, same sterile double gloves, same virtual operating room, same fully draped adult training patient, same surgeon positioned on the technician's front-right side, same Mayo stand directly in front-left, same back table farther left, same anesthesia monitor beyond the patient's head, same open-appendectomy instrument set, same instrument order, same cool clinical lighting, same teal-and-deep-navy commercial color grade. During the virtual procedure the camera remains at the technician's natural eye height in first-person view, with subtle human head movement only. The technician's hands remain inside the sterile working area, in front of the torso and above waist level when not actively handling an instrument. Hands never fall to the sides, touch the mask, face, headset, nonsterile equipment or anything below waist level. The surgeon performs all operative actions; the technician never operates on the patient. Photorealistic materials, anatomically correct hands, restrained motion, credible operating-room teamwork and premium medical-training-platform aesthetics. Each scene begins from the exact final frame and sound state of the preceding scene. Do not generate readable text.

## 4. Global Sound Continuity

Use one continuous soundscape across all eight scenes:

- Low, clean operating-room room tone
- Soft ventilation and distant equipment hum
- Stable cardiac monitor tone synchronized with the visible ECG rhythm
- Calm anesthesia ventilator inhale/exhale cycle synchronized with subtle rise and fall beneath the upper drape
- Quiet stainless-steel instrument sounds only when a tool is touched
- Subtle Arqenion interface chimes; no arcade or gaming sounds
- Calm team voices at realistic room volume; no shouting or urgency
- Music begins as a restrained low electronic pulse and gradually becomes warmer and more aspirational toward the final brand frame

Suggested stable training values for post-production graphics only:

`HR 74 BPM / SpO₂ 99% / RR 14 MIN⁻¹ / STABLE`

# 7. Editing and Generation Notes

1. **Generate as adjacent clips, edit as one shot.** Give each new generation the preceding scene's final frame as its visual reference whenever the tool supports image-to-video or continuation.
2. **Lock the room layout.** Mayo stand front-left, surgeon front-right, patient center, anesthesia monitor upper-right/background and back table left must never swap sides.
3. **Lock instrument positions.** Save a clean still of the table from Scene 05 and reuse it as the layout reference for Scene 07.
4. **Do not ask the model to render typography.** Add all labels, numbers and the logo in editing.
5. **Keep the UI secondary.** The product should enhance technician awareness, not turn the OR into a holographic game.
6. **Synchronize three layers in post:** ECG peak, monitor beep and performance pulse. Synchronize ventilator audio with the subtle drape rise-and-fall.
7. **Use the actual institutional preference card before final release.** Instrument sets and local role boundaries vary by institution; adapt the table and labels to the approved curriculum.
8. **Clinical review is required.** Before publishing, have an experienced scrub practitioner and the institution's perioperative educator review hand position, sterile boundaries, table layout and instrument-transfer behavior.
